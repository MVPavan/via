# Focused interviewing

Ask about material missing intent, behavior or constraints. Reuse supplied answers
and available facts. A recommendation can make a choice concrete; label its
assumptions and avoid deliberately misleading guesses to elicit disagreement.

Batch independent questions when manageable. Ask dependent questions after their
prerequisites are answered. Translate vague goals such as scalable or simple into
observable outcomes only when that distinction affects the design.

Interpret approval in context. A clear acceptance of a concrete proposal or an
explicit delegation of routine choices is usable authority; do not reject it
merely because the user did not repeat an option's name. Silence is not approval.
Ask again only when scope, correctness or authority remains materially ambiguous.

If successive questions produce no new information, explain the unresolved
choice and reframe it. Stop when the requested decisions are sufficient for the
next step, not when every hypothetical branch has been visited.
