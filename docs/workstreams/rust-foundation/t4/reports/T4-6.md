## Deviations

- **Spec text vs design.** C1 §3.10/§3.11 (`docs/specs/via-api-v1.md`)
  still says an individual result too large for the page is
  `admission_refused`. Design §4.3 [t4r17.8] says it is a debug assertion,
  not a refusal. The code follows the design; the spec line is for the
  orchestrator.
- **Test placement.**
  - The `events` byte bound and the `list` 1000-row examination are tested
    at the Store level (`s1_c1_pages.rs`). A daemon cannot cheaply create
    1,250 sessions or 250 KiB events. The daemon tests cover the C1
    surface.
  - The envelope-at-maximum test builds the envelope through Core's own
    `terminal_envelope` via the test-only hook `via_core::envelope_at_maximum`.
    The fake route has no denial or decline message, and adding one would
    change the fake protocol.
  - Its receipt half runs against the daemon.
- **`types`** accepts only the 16 durable types of design §2.1. Each name
  is matched as it is decoded and repeats collapse, so a 65,000-node list
  allocates nothing per name. An unknown name or an empty list is
  `invalid_params`. Reason: the Store Public lane refuses requests over
  4 KiB, which an unchecked list would reach.
- **`list` filters.** `invalid_params` for any of these:
  - a `state` other than `active`, `idle` or `closed`;
  - a `harness` over 1 KiB;
  - a `label` over 120 B;
  - a `since` that is not `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SS.mmmZ`.
- **`events` `next_after`.** Stopped by the byte bound: the seq of the
  first row left out, minus 1. Stopped at `limit`: the last returned seq.
  Otherwise `min(after + 1000, head)`, never below `after`.
- **Final-text failure.** Recorded as the turn's first failure (site
  `Event`, `NotCommitted`), as a refused step row is. That sends the store
  stop order, and the terminal is `failed(store)` with the message "the
  final text could not be written". Being scoped, it does not latch the
  daemon, but `daemon/status` `store_failure` reports it.
- **Final text after a spill.** A spilled text is never inline again: when
  the file cannot be named (sync failure), both `final_text` and
  `final_text_file` are `null`.
- **Final text of forced turns.** A turn handed to final shutdown keeps
  `final_text: ""` and names no file. A file it already created stays
  unnamed in its folder, where `logs` lists it.
- **Receipt maxima.**
  - `model` (encoded > 1 KiB) is checked in `check_session_members`
    (spawn; `resume` refuses `model` anyway).
  - `bound`, `vendor` and `effort` are checked there and at the top of
    `PerTurn::fake_overrides` (spawn and resume).
  - All are `Named::field`, with no `route`.
- **Edit outside owned paths.** `.config/nextest.toml` gains an override
  that runs `s1_f24_flood_*` alone (`threads-required =
  "num-test-threads"`):
  - Before it, 3 of 4 full failpoint runs had one unrelated timing
    failure, and so did 1 of 2 runs without F24 (see Concerns).
  - With it, three consecutive full runs were green
    (`gate-nextest-failpoints-5/6.log`, `gate-G-final.log`).
  - Cost: the failpoint suite takes about 67 s instead of 32 s.

## Concerns and limits

- **Pre-existing load flakes.** Each of these failed once in full-suite runs
  and passed alone (6 of 6 for the first):
  - `s1_close_partial_restarts_keep_one_result`
  - `s1_close_outcome_retained_for_late_subscriber_under_force`
  - `restart_handoff::starts_beyond_the_channel_spill_…`
  - `s1_shutdown_budget_read_cutoff_before_reconciliation`

  One occurred with F24 excluded
  (`gate-nextest-failpoints-nof24-2.log`), so they predate this chunk. F24's
  load made them more likely until it ran alone.
- **F24 growth measurement.**
  - Without a warm-up, growth after 64 MiB was 24–39 MiB (one run over
    32 MiB).
  - A run with the flood alone (no sockets, `exp-nosockets-*.log`) showed
    flat or falling RSS through the flood. The growth came from the
    29-socket request load ramping up (allocator arenas), not from the
    flood.
  - The test therefore lets the socket load reach 16 answered lines per
    socket before the flood. This choice of measurement should be
    reviewed. No limit was enlarged.
- **F24 scope.**
  - Not driven to their maximum: the Adapter's observation channel (text
    items cost 512 B of its 4 MiB) and `events` pages (the read session
    has three events).
  - The flooding turns fail `overflow` on a final 2 MiB line, because a
    paced flood never overflows Wire's queue.
  - Controls were timed on a 32-core machine; a slow machine may approach
    100 ms.
- **Untested seam.** `final_text.write.fail` exists but no test arms it;
  `write.short` and `sync.fail` are tested.
- **Unexercised list code.** Envelope lists have no producer on the fake
  route. `Kept::push` runs only under the test hook and is
  `expect(dead_code)` outside test builds.
- **T4-7 overlap in the receipt tests.** `s1_bounds_envelope_…` asserts
  that `model` of 1 KiB passes the size check, meaning it is not refused
  as `invalid_params` naming `model` without a route. Today an unknown
  model is a plain `invalid_params` with no `field`. If T4-7's
  `UNKNOWN_MODEL` names `model` via `Named::field`, that assertion needs
  the new kind at merge. Both chunks also add hunks to `api.rs`
  (`check_session_members`, `fake_overrides` and the DTO section).
- **Debug cost.** The `ENVELOPE_MAX` assertion serializes each envelope a
  second time, in debug builds only.

### Findings and fixes

1. **F24 growth baseline** (important; 0d96e81). The growth check used the
   highest RSS sample before 64 MiB as its baseline. It now uses the RSS
   of the **last sample (in time order) with `flooded < 64 MiB`**, so any
   later rise counts. The metric is renamed `rss_at_64_mib_kib`. The socket
   warm-up and the peak-over-idle check are unchanged.
2. **Pid-checked readiness and the child's pid** (important; 0d96e81).
   - Every T4-6 daemon scenario (`s1_bounds`, `s1_c1_reads`,
     `s1_f24_memory`) starts through `Daemon::start`/`start_with`, so it
     reaches readiness only through the pid-checked probe.
   - The new `Daemon::pid()` returns the child's pid, the one readiness
     matched; the readiness loop itself uses it.
   - The `s1_bounds` crash scenario (`wait_ack` and `SIGKILL`) and the F24
     RSS sampler use `daemon.pid()`. `Setup::daemon_pid` and the F24
     `via daemon status` pid lookup are removed.
3. **`list_page` assertion** (minor; a75d28a). The byte-bound break
   carries `debug_assert!(bytes > 0, "the first summary always fits a
   page")`, matching `events_page` (A49).
4. **`failure.message` quotes** (minor; 585f09b). The cut is now
   `FAILURE_MESSAGE_MAX - 2` escaped bytes, so the message with its quotes
   stays within 2 KiB. The unit test
   `failure_message_encodes_within_two_kib_with_its_quotes` covers ASCII,
   `"` and `é` messages.
   - RED (`r1-red-failure-message.log`): panicked with `2050 bytes
     encoded`.
   - GREEN (`r1-green-failure-message.log`): 1 passed.
5. **`events` after `i64::MAX`** (minor; a75d28a). `read_events_page`
   returns an empty page `{next_after: after, more: false}` for any
   `after >= head`. The check runs after the session and turn existence
   checks and before the i64 conversion. The new Store test
   `s1_c1_events_page_after_above_i64_is_past_the_head` covers `i64::MAX`,
   `i64::MAX + 1` and `u64::MAX`, with and without a turn filter.
   - RED (`r1-red-events-after.log`): `Constraint("event sequence too
     large")`.
   - GREEN (`r1-green-events-after.log`): 3 of 3 passed.
6. **Seam `final_text.write.fail`** (measurement; 5681794). The final-text
   scenario arms it: the first write fails before any byte. The turn is
   `failed(store)` with `final_text: null`, and `final_text_file` names the
   empty 0600 file with `bytes: 0` and `truncated: true`.
   - GREEN: `r1-final-text-write-fail.log`.
   - With the point renamed in the Store (unwired), the turn completes and
     the check fails (`r1-final-text-write-fail-unwired.log`).
