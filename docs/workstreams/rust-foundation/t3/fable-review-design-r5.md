## Findings

**Major 1 (S1, via-host, decision 2). A `Stop` sent to a pre-ARM control kills the anchor with an error and mislabels the turn.** `design.md:866-877` registers a control at verification, before ARM, and has the early stop send `Stop` to every snapshotted control. The anchor's pre-ARM loop accepts only Configure, Arm, Challenge and Status. Anything else is `invalid pre-arm control` and the anchor exits 1 (`crates/via-host/src/anchor.rs:151`). Scenario: a control is registered, force fires, the snapshot includes it, and the early stop writes `Stop` while the acquisition is between `Configure` and `Arm` (`crates/via-host/src/host.rs:575-605`). The anchor exits, the acquisition's next transact fails with an I/O or protocol error, not `HostError::Stopped`, and Wire maps that to `WireError::Host` (`crates/via-wire/src/runtime.rs:225-231`) unless the turn's own stop watch is already raised. A run loop blocked on Store, the case decision 3 exists for, has not raised it. The turn ends `failed(transport)` instead of `cancelled`. If the pre-ARM stream is not yet behind the shared mutex, the frames also interleave. Fix: give the ledger entry a phase, `verified`, `arming`, `armed`. The early stop sends `Stop` only to `armed` entries. The owner's ARM gate reads `stopping` under the ledger mutex, atomic with the snapshot, and refuses with `HostError::Stopped` and the EOF drop of row 4. The owner marks `armed` under the ledger mutex immediately after `Spawned`, before `commit_vendor_facts`, and sends `Stop` itself under the original deadline if `stopping` is set. Any acquisition failure after `stopping` was observed returns `HostError::Stopped`. Test 1552 must then pause at the snapshot while a turn's ARM completes and expect `host.early_stop.sent`; a pre-ARM variant expects `Stopped { launched: false }` and no `Stop` frame.

**Major 2 (S1, via-host, decision 1). The early-stop task can block idle exit forever.** `design.md:894-901` makes the task a Host-owned task that lives for the daemon's lifetime and is joined by `Host::shutdown`. `design.md:710-715` makes idle exit require "no Host control or close task still running", read through the pending-cleanup accessor S1 also owns. The only Host task registry today is `HostTasks.running` (`crates/via-host/src/host.rs:79-87`), which already holds the per-group reaper and poll tasks. Scenario: S1 derives the accessor from that registry, the early-stop task is always in it, `idle_since` never sets, and every daemon lives until an explicit stop. The origin is round 4's task placement, but round 5 rewrote the Lifetime bullet without settling it. Fix: state under Lifetime that the early-stop task is excluded from the pending-cleanup count and the idle predicate, and that only the shutdown join counts it.

**Minor 3 (S1 test 1548, decision 4). The withholding fixture must also defer cleanup, or reconciliation's `Stop` has no anchor to answer it.** The real anchor begins cleanup on the first `Stop` and kills its own group within 200 ms (`crates/via-host/src/anchor.rs:200, 232`). `design.md:1445` withholds only the reply. Scenario: the early stop's `Stop` triggers cleanup, the group is gone within a second, reconciliation's `Stop` at step 4 gets EOF, `stopped_live` is never delivered, and the test cannot pass. Fix: the seam defers `begin_cleanup` until the Stop it identifies as reconciliation's, by count, and the row says so. Route's close then reports `uncertain` and reconciliation supplies both facts, which is the ordering the test wants.

**Minor 4 (S1 unit test 1561, decision 6). The classification row still says "`try_send` `Full` gives `NotEnqueued`" without "the SQLite writer's".** The later clause makes raw `Full` `Raw`, so an implementer can read the row two ways. This is the ambiguity Astra flagged. Fix: qualify the first clause.

**Minor 5 (S1 test 1553, decision 3). "Held busy" and "sent without waiting" have no named witness.** `design.md:1553` gives only a `now + 3 s` bound, which is a timing assertion. Fix: hold the busy control's `Stop` at a named pause in the fixture anchor and assert the other group's `host.early_stop.sent` acknowledgement arrives while that pause is still held.

**Minor 6 (S2 test 1550, decision 8). The pause between order check and subscription has no seam in §10.** The pause sits under `admission`, and `request_stop` takes `admission` (`crates/via-core/src/engine/stop.rs:90`), so the force must already be accepted when the caller enters and the racing publication must be a force or latch exit, which takes no `admission`. The step-2 order check precedes the step-4 fence, so the caller does reach it. Fix: name the seam and state both constraints in the row.

**Minor 7 (S3, decision 10). The "one table" has two rows claiming the last 2 s.** `design.md:834-851` ends `FINALIZE_RESERVE` at `deadline` and ends client joins at `deadline − 2 s`, but `final_shutdown` joins clients only after `engine.shutdown` returns (`crates/via-cli/src/server/shutdown.rs:79-82`). A pipeline that uses its reserve leaves the client join no time and a pending `wait` on a committed record is cut. The exit stays truthful. Fix: state that client joins and the Store join run after the pipeline and take whatever remains, and that a pipeline past `deadline − 2 s` already exits 4.

## Answers

**1. Decisions.** Decisions 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11 and 12 are implemented as decided. Decision 2 is present but unsafe pre-ARM (finding 1). Decision 6 is now consistent across §7.1, row 6, A14 and the unit test apart from finding 4.

**2. Races and waits.** The sticky `stopping` flag and the snapshot share the ledger mutex alone, never nested, so no cycle with `admission`, a head or `stop`. Concurrent stops touch distinct controls; on one control the stream mutex serialises the early stop, Route and reconciliation, and `Stop` is idempotent, so no double writer post-ARM. The retained `StopFacts.forced` is write-once and already read by `Host::shutdown` (`host.rs:730-734`). The shutdown signal removes the unbounded wait on stop and drain, and its wake is safe if it is a watch or a permit-keeping `Notify`, which §1's rule already requires. Per-generation close outcomes remove the lost wake; `admission` held across the order check and subscription prevents a new generation from resetting the value underneath a waiter. The close pass's force select and step-5 re-check are ordered by `admission` against `request_stop`. Entry on the force signal and phase two both take `admission`, in either order. The collect-release-insert keeps `force_sessions` unnested; nothing enqueues between collect and insert because `request_stop` holds `admission`. Connection slots are still released only by proofs. Re-probe is untouched by the early stop. The read cutoff at `deadline − 8 s` leaves reconciliation about 2 s after Route's 3 s force close, bounded and truthful.

**3. Tests.** Tests 1547, 1549, 1551 and 1554 prove their orderings through acknowledgements and bound checks, with no sleeps. Test 1548 depends on finding 3. Test 1550 needs the seam of finding 6. Test 1552 asserts the wrong witness for a pre-ARM registration (finding 1). Test 1553 has no witness for its concurrency claim (finding 5).
