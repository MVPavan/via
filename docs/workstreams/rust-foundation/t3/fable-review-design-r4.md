**Verdict: SOUND WITH CHANGES.** All 9 round-4 decisions are present in `design.md` at 1a814d6 where the Round 4 map says they are. Decision 3's early-stop task is specified with a lifetime defect that turns every idle and drain shutdown into exit 4, and the new 5 s reserve collides with an existing 4 s constant. ## Findings

**Blocker 1 (S1, via-host). The early-stop task never finishes on an idle or drain shutdown, so every clean shutdown exits 4 after a 5 s stall.** `design.md:851-852` says the task is "joined by Host's shutdown and counted in its pending or failed tasks, like other Host tasks", and its only wake is the force watch (`:853-854`). On idle or drain exit the force watch never fires and the Engine holds its sender until after `Host::shutdown`. Scenario: idle expiry, `Engine::shutdown` calls `adapter.shutdown` with `host_by = deadline − 5 s`, `join_owned_tasks` (`crates/via-host/src/host.rs:922-965`) waits on the blocked task until `host_by`, reports `pending_tasks: 1` and `failure: Deadline`, and `is_clean()` is false. Fix: the task selects on the force watch or a Host-owned stop signal; `Host::shutdown` raises that signal before joining, and the task exits immediately when it has nothing to stop. State this under Lifetime.

**Major 2 (S3, via-core). `FINALIZE_RESERVE = 5 s` is larger than the force-path read cutoff's 4 s, so a dispatcher that reads to its cutoff leaves reconciliation zero time.** `design.md:813-823` sets reconciliation to run until `deadline − 5 s`. `crates/via-core/src/engine/latch.rs:17` sets `READ_RETRY_RESERVE = 4 s`, so a force-path read (§6.7) may retry until `deadline − 4 s` and the dispatcher joins after `host_by`. `Host::shutdown` then returns at once with `HostError::Deadline` (`host.rs:711-720`), every forced turn gets `cleanup: uncertain` because `quiescent` falls back to `report.failure.is_none()` (`stop.rs:180-184`), their connection slots are never released, and the exit is 4. Round 3's 1 s reserve left 3 s here. Fix: state one budget table in §6.8 and set the read cutoff at `deadline − (FINALIZE_RESERVE + 3 s)`, naming `latch.rs:17` as a changed constant.

**Major 3 (S5 test, S1 seam). `s1_f12_host_early_stop_independent_of_store` cannot reach its precondition as written.** `design.md:1484` pauses turn B at `store.commit.event` and then latches through turn A's `store.commit.reply_lost`. Both seams are writer-thread points (`store.commit.reply_lost` is at `crates/via-store/src/runtime/sql.rs:298`), and a `pause` at a synchronous `hit` blocks the single writer thread. A's write queues behind B's pause, `reply_lost` never fires, and the latch never happens; if the 2 s watchdog rescues A, it rescues B at the same instant, so the test cannot tell independent stopping from Route polling after B unblocked. Fix: pause B on the Core side before its send (a `core.commit.before_send` seam, or a Store seam that commits and parks only the reply), keep the writer free for A, and assert the `host.early_stop.sent` acknowledgement timestamp precedes B's release acknowledgement.

**Major 4 (S1, via-host). The early-stop task's reply is discarded, so `forced` evidence is lost exactly in the case test 1485 exercises.** `design.md:846-849` says the task "never settles a turn and never commits evidence". `forced` reaches reconciliation only through `StopFacts.forced` (`host.rs:731-733`), which `ProcessControl::close` sets from the anchor's `Stopping{stopped_live}` reply (`host.rs:1030-1033`); at reconciliation the control stream is usually dropped, so no second Stop is sent. Scenario (test `:1485`): force; the early stop's Stop finds the vendor live; Route's own Stop reply is lost at `host.anchor.final_reply_lost`; reconciliation proves absence but has no `forced` fact; the terminal is `unknown` with `cleanup: quiescent`, not `cancelled`. Fix: the task records the anchor's `stopped_live` in the control's stop facts, which is in-memory evidence rather than a commit. The anchor repeats the original `stopped_live` on later Stops (`crates/via-host/src/anchor.rs:201, 310-326`), so ordering between the two stops does not matter.

**Major 5 (S1, via-wire and via-routes). Raw `WriterLost` has no named latch path.** `design.md:908-913` and row 6 (`:965`) make raw `Disconnected` and a dropped raw reply latch, but the raw error travels Wire → Route → Core as `WireError::Raw(_)` → `RouteError::Store { turn }` (`crates/via-routes/src/runtime.rs:397`), which carries no kind. Core then runs row 6's scoped resolution for a dead raw thread, turn after turn, with `health: healthy`, the exact failure decision 5 closed. Fix: Wire reports the classified kind on `RouteError::Store` (or a `latch: bool`) and Core latches on `WriterLost` and `Uncertain`, scoping only `Raw` and `NotEnqueued`; name that in §7.1's raw rules.

**Major 6 (S2). A close waiter that subscribes after the publication still hangs.** `design.md:109-113` claims a value published between release and await is never missed, but a tokio `watch::Sender::subscribe()` marks the current value as seen, so a waiter that subscribes after the publication waits for a change that never comes. Scenario: force accepted; the dispatcher publishes `daemon_stopping` and calls `slot.stop()` (`:545-547`) without clearing the close order; a keyed replay of that close arrives, bypasses the fence through step 2's "close in progress" branch (`:457-459`), subscribes, and hangs until the client abort at the final deadline. Fix: the close watch holds `Option<outcome>` and waiters use `wait_for(Option::is_some)` semantics (the current value counts), and the force and latch exits clear the close order under slot state before `slot.stop()`.

**Minor 7 (S3). Entry "at the latch's phase two" (`design.md:780, 1100`) is not how daemon main wakes.** It wakes on the force watch (`crates/via-cli/src/server.rs:143`), which phase one raises. Entry and phase two both take `admission` in either order, and `store_failed()` already includes `failure_pending`, so I found no race. Fix: say entry is on the force signal, and that phase two may complete before or after it.

**Minor 8 (S1, via-host). A control registered after the early-stop snapshot is missed.** `design.md:835-838` snapshots the ledger once. Between the pre-ARM gate (`host.rs:591-594`) and `track_control` (`host.rs:636`) sits an awaited `commit_vendor_facts`; a force in that window leaves a live vendor that only Route's poll will stop. Fix: `track_control` re-reads the force watch after inserting the control and sends Stop itself.

**Minor 9 (S1, via-host). Sequential Stops share one 3 s bound.** `design.md:836-838` sends per control in turn; each Stop takes the control's stream mutex, which an in-flight Route `transact` may hold for up to 3 s against a hung anchor. Fix: send concurrently, each bounded at `now + 3 s`.

**Minor 10 (S2 test). `s1_close_waiter_resolves_on_force_and_latch` (`design.md:1486`) assumes a force aborts a close pass already at its absence check.** §4's dispatcher steps 4 and 5 never say they re-check force, so an implementation that finishes the absence check and commits `Closed` replies with the close result, which is truthful and fails the test. Fix: either state that the absence check selects on the force watch and step 5 re-checks force under `admission`, or let the test accept either outcome and assert only that no waiter is left.

**Minor 11 (S3). The new mutex rule contradicts today's `request_stop`.** `design.md:72-76` forbids nesting `force_sessions` with another `std` lock; `crates/via-core/src/engine/stop.rs:118-119` takes `force_sessions` then `sessions`, and holds `stop` while it computes the mode. Fix: state that the force set is collected under `sessions` and slot state, released, then inserted, and that the plain-stop closing-set check runs under `admission` before `stop` is taken.

**Minor 12 (S3). The reserve arithmetic (`design.md:815-819`) covers one re-read, one commit and the closure pass.** With two or more forced turns or batch turns, later commits are cut at the deadline and count in `uncommitted_turns` by construction. That is bounded, not unbounded, but the text should say the reserve is per pipeline, not per turn.

## Answers

1. **Decisions.** 1, 2, 4, 7, 8 and 9 are implemented as decided. 3 is implemented with findings 1, 4, 8 and 9. 5 is implemented in the text and A14/A17, with the latch path unnamed (finding 5). 6 is implemented with the subscription gap (finding 6).
2. **States and transitions.** The reordered pipeline, single control owner and idempotent Stop introduce no double writer: the control stream's mutex serialises Route, the poll task and the early stop, and the anchor's `begin_cleanup` runs once. The early stop takes only the ledger mutex and cannot deadlock against `admission` or a head. Against stop and drain the early stop is inert except for finding 1. Against force and the latch the pipeline order is right, and the hand-off barrier still holds. Against the latch the `stop`/force publication of phase one and `admission` of phase two are unchanged. Connection slots are released only by proofs, which finding 2 can prevent. Re-probe and restart are unaffected. Findings 1, 2, 4 and 5 are untruthful outcomes; finding 6 is a lost wake.
3. **Seams and tests.** The fence seams, `core.head.contended`, `core.shutdown.reconcile_entry` and `host.early_stop.sent` are proper ordering witnesses, and tests 1418, 1420, 1421, 1481 and 1483 prove their orderings without fixed sleeps. Test 1484 cannot establish its precondition (finding 3), and test 1485 depends on finding 4.
