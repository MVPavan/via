## Step 2: design note (`t3/design.md`)

Normative, concise sentences; no tutorial. Cover only gaps that add states
or change failure behaviour, at minimum:

- `cancel` of a queued and of a running turn, and the C1 §7.4 outcome
  evidence (acknowledgement vs cleanup vs `unknown`);
- `close` and the session `closing` state: its interaction with queued
  turns, a running turn, drain, force and restart;
- the Core idle deadline alongside the frozen wall deadline;
- the F12 remainder, force closure of idle sessions, the outstanding
  Store read after the force cutoff, and persistent queued-row read
  failure;
- the re-probe loop for unproven or recovered groups and unread anchors;
- F6 (idle exit racing a new client) and F22 (restart kills an orphan only
  after uid, start time, group and marker match), if not already
  implemented.

For each: the states added; lock order against `dispatch-design.md`
(`stop`, `admission`, the event head) and wakes; interaction with stop,
force, the Store-failed latch, connection slots and restart handoff; any
schema change (schema v5 keeps the pre-release refusal of older dev
Stores); and the failure-first tests that prove it. Where a rule in
`dispatch-design.md` or a contract must change, flag it explicitly as an
amendment rather than editing around it.

Decisions already made; do not reopen:

- Runtime §7 as written: the first failed or uncertain Core state write
  latches, and the daemon force-stops and exits 4. There is no in-daemon
  orphan reconciler; restart recovery and keyed replay settle uncertainty.
  (The owner may later allow surviving a one-off write failure. If your
  design would differ materially under that alternative, list the points in

- Deadlines are Core-owned and absolute. A grandchild that calls `setsid`
  escapes group cleanup; S1 documents that limit and does not solve it.
- No new dependencies, debug RPCs or CLI verbs beyond C1's.
